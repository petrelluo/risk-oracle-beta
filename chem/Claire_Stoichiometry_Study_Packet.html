2. Conversion Factors You Must Know Cold

  • 1 mole = molar mass in grams (add up atomic masses from the periodic table)
  • 1 mole = 6.02 × 1023 particles (atoms, molecules, or formula units)
  • 1 mole gas = 22.4 L at STP
  • Mole ratio = the ratio of coefficients in the balanced equation

3. Vocabulary (from the worksheet)

  • Limiting Reagent — the reactant used up first. This determines how much product is made.
  • Excess Reagent — the reactant that has some leftover when the reaction is complete. You had more than enough.
  • Theoretical yield — the amount of product stoichiometry predicts (the math answer).
  • Experimental (actual) yield — what was collected in the lab.
  • Percent yield — a measure of how efficient the reaction was.

4. Limiting Reagent — Dr. Pangrle's 3-Step Process

Per the class worksheet, whenever amounts are given for both reactants, follow these three steps exactly:

Step 1 Convert each reactant to the same product (mol or g) using the Mole Roadmap. Pick one product; run both reactants to it.
Step 2 Compare. Whichever reactant gave the smaller amount is the limiting reagent. The other is in excess.
Step 3 Use the limiting reagent only for any further math — actual product, OR how much of the excess was used.
When only one reactant amount is given If a problem says "reacted with excess" something, the substance with the given amount is automatically the limiting reagent. No comparison needed — just run the roadmap.

5. Excess Reagent Leftover

After identifying the limiting reagent, use it to calculate how much of the excess reagent was used up by running the limiting reagent through the roadmap toward the excess reagent. Then:

leftover = initial amount of excess − amount used up

6. Percent Yield

The theoretical yield is what stoichiometry predicts. The experimental yield is what was actually collected.

percent yield = (experimental yield ÷ theoretical yield) × 100

Why yields fall short: reactants weren't 100% pure · product gets lost during transfer · high temperatures cause vaporization · side reactions consume reactant.

Worked ExamplesThe two worksheet problems · Dr. Pangrle's exact method

These are the exact problems from the "Limiting & Excess Reagents" worksheet. Study these carefully — they show the precise format and method the teacher expects on MQ8C.

EXAMPLE 1  ·  CS2 + 3 O2 → CO2 + 2 SO2

Given: 1.0 mol CS2 and 1.0 mol O2

STEP 1 — Convert each reactant to mol CO2
From CS2: 1.0 mol CS2 × (1 mol CO2 / 1 mol CS2) = 1.0 mol CO2
From O2:  1.0 mol O2 × (1 mol CO2 / 3 mol O2) = 0.33 mol CO2
STEP 2 — Compare
0.33 < 1.0, so O2 produces less. O2 is limiting. CS2 is in excess.
PART (a) — Limiting reagent
O2 is limiting
PART (b) — Moles of CS2 remaining
CS2 used: 1.0 mol O2 × (1 mol CS2 / 3 mol O2) = 0.33 mol CS2
Leftover: 1.0 − 0.33 = 0.7 mol CS2 remain
PART (c) — Grams of CO2 formed (use limiting!)
1.0 mol O2 × (1 mol CO2 / 3 mol O2) × (44.01 g / 1 mol) = 15 g CO2
EXAMPLE 2  ·  Mg + 2 H2O → Mg(OH)2 + H2

Given: 16.2 g Mg and 12.0 g H2O

STEP 1 — Convert each reactant to g H2
From Mg:  16.2 g Mg × (1 mol / 24.31 g) × (1 mol H2 / 1 mol Mg) × (2.02 g / mol) = 1.34 g H2
From H2O: 12.0 g H2O × (1 mol / 18.02 g) × (1 mol H2 / 2 mol H2O) × (2.02 g / mol) = 0.671 g H2
STEP 2 — Compare
0.671 < 1.34, so H2O produces less. H2O is limiting. Mg is in excess.
PART (a) — Limiting reagent
H2O is limiting
PART (b) — Grams of Mg remaining
Mg used: 12.0 g H2O × (1 mol / 18.02 g) × (1 mol Mg / 2 mol H2O) × (24.31 g / mol) = 8.1 g Mg
Leftover: 16.2 − 8.1 = 8.1 g Mg remain
Notice the pattern Whatever final unit you pick for Step 1 (mol of product, or grams of product), use the same unit for both reactants so the comparison is fair. The worksheet used mol CO2 in Example 1 and grams H2 in Example 2 — either works.

Formulas to Memorize

Percent Yield

% yield = (actual / theoretical) × 100
Actual = experimental (lab). Theoretical = stoichiometry.

Excess Leftover

leftover = initial − used
Run the limiting reagent toward the excess to find "used".
Common pitfalls Forgetting to balance · flipping the mole ratio upside down · skipping the LR check when both amounts are given · using atomic mass instead of molar mass for compounds · no units written next to numbers.

Quiz A — Full Chapter

1. 0.50 mol N2 × (2 mol NH3 / 1 mol N2) = 1.0 mol NH3
2. 25.0 g CaCO3 × (1 mol / 100.09 g) × (1 mol CaO / 1 mol CaCO3) × (56.08 g / 1 mol) = 14.0 g CaO
3. Step 1 — convert each to g H2:
From Zn: 5.0 × (1/65.38) × (1 mol H2 / 1 mol Zn) × 2.02 = 0.154 g H2
From HCl: 5.0 × (1/36.46) × (1 mol H2 / 2 mol HCl) × 2.02 = 0.138 g H2
Step 2: 0.138 < 0.154 → HCl is limiting   Step 3: g H2 = 0.138 g H2
4. Step 1 — convert each to mol Al2O3:
From Al: 4.0 × (2 mol Al2O3 / 4 mol Al) = 2.00 mol Al2O3
From O2: 4.0 × (2/3) = 2.67 mol Al2O3
Step 2: 2.00 < 2.67 → (a) Al is limiting
Step 3: O2 used = 4.0 mol Al × (3 mol O2 / 4 mol Al) = 3.0 mol. Leftover = 4.0 − 3.0 = (b) 1.0 mol O2
5. Theoretical: 5.0 g H2 × (1/2.02) × (2 mol NH3 / 3 mol H2) × 17.04 = 28.2 g NH3
% yield = (20.0 / 28.2) × 100 = 71.0 %

Quiz B — Full Chapter

1. 3.0 mol O2 × (2 mol H2O / 1 mol O2) = 6.0 mol H2O
2. 50.0 g Al × (1/26.98) × (2 mol Al2O3 / 4 mol Al) × (6.02×1023 / 1 mol) = 5.58 × 1023 molecules Al2O3
3. Step 1 — convert each to mol CO2:
From CS2: 1.0 × (1/1) = 1.0 mol CO2. From O2: 1.0 × (1/3) = 0.33 mol CO2.
Step 2: 0.33 < 1.0 → (a) O2 is limiting
(b) CS2 used = 1.0 mol O2 × (1/3) = 0.33 mol CS2. Leftover = 0.67 mol CS2
(c) g CO2 = 1.0 mol O2 × (1/3) × 44.01 = 14.7 g CO2 (≈ 15 g)
4. Step 1 — convert each to mol H2O:
From H2: 5.0/2.02 × (2/2) = 2.48 mol H2O. From O2: 8.0/32.00 × (2/1) = 0.50 mol H2O.
Step 2: 0.50 < 2.48 → (a) O2 is limiting
(b) H2 used: 8.0/32.00 × (2/1) × 2.02 = 1.01 g H2. Leftover = 5.0 − 1.01 = 4.0 g H2
5. Theoretical: 10.00 g Fe × (1/55.85) × (1 mol Fe2S3 / 2 mol Fe) × 207.91 = 18.61 g Fe2S3
% yield = (16.50 / 18.61) × 100 = 88.7 %

Quiz E — Full Chapter · Mastery

1. Four mole ratios involving CS2 in CS2 + 3 O2 → CO2 + 2 SO2:
1 mol CS2 / 3 mol O2  and inverse  3 mol O2 / 1 mol CS2

(Also acceptable: 1 mol CS2 / 2 mol SO2 and its inverse.)
2. 2.20 g CO2 × (1 mol / 44.01 g) × (1 mol C / 1 mol CO2) × (6.02×1023 / 1 mol) = 3.01 × 1022 atoms C
3. Step 1 — convert each to g CO2:
From C3H8: 94.3/44.11 × 3 × 44.01 = 282 g CO2.
From O2: 185/32.00 × (3/5) × 44.01 = 153 g CO2.
Step 2: 153 < 282 → O2 is limiting, C3H8 is in excess

From Fe: 5.0/55.85 × (1/2) × 207.91 = 9.31 g Fe2S3.
From S: 5.0/32.07 × (1/3) × 207.91 = 10.81 g Fe2S3.
Step 2: 9.31 < 10.81 → (a) Fe is limiting   (b) 9.31 g Fe2S3
5. Step 1 — convert each to g H2:
From Mg: 15.0/24.31 × (1/1) × 2.02 = 1.24 g H2.
From H2O: 20.0/18.02 × (1/2) × 2.02 = 1.12 g H2.
Step 2: 1.12 < 1.24 → (a) H2O is limiting
(b) Theoretical = 1.12 g H2
(c) % yield = (1.00 / 1.12) × 100 = 89.3 %

  • Are your conversion factors arranged so units cancel diagonally?
